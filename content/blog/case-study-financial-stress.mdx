This site lives next to a real machine-learning project: the **Financial Stress
Prediction challenge**, with a full pipeline notebook (`financial_stress_pipeline.ipynb`)
in the repository root. During Phase 6 we turn that project into your capstone.

## Why a tabular case study first

LLMs get the headlines, but most industry data science is tabular: loans,
churn, claims, risk. The capstone exercises the *whole* pipeline:

1. **Framing** — turning "predict financial stress" into a supervised problem
   with a leakage-safe target and metric.
2. **EDA** — distributions, missingness, target leakage suspects.
3. **Feature engineering** — ratios, binned income bands, one-hot vs ordinal
   encoding.
4. **Modeling** — logistic regression baseline → gradient boosting
   (see [XGBoost: A Scalable Tree Boosting System](/papers)).
5. **Evaluation** — stratified cross-validation, ROC-AUC vs PR-AUC on an
   imbalanced target, calibration.

<Callout kind="deep" title="Leakage is the story">
  The most instructive part of a stress-prediction dataset is how easily
  *outcome-adjacent* columns sneak into training. The capstone day walks through
  hunting them down — it's the skill interviewers probe hardest.
</Callout>

## What "done" looks like

By Day 60 you'll have:

- a clean, reproducible notebook (and a refactored Python package),
- a model card documenting intended use and failure modes,
- a deployed demo you can link on your CV,
- and this write-up as the first serious entry in your portfolio blog.

The math you'll lean on: logistic regression's log-odds form,

$$\log \frac{p(y=1 \mid x)}{1 - p(y=1 \mid x)} = w^\top x + b,$$

covered in the probability track, and the evaluation theory from Days 20–21.
