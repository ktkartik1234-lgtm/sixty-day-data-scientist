Most "learn data science" advice dies in tutorial hell. This project is built
around one fix: **a fixed 60-day path, one day at a time, with the math and the
papers attached to the days that need them.**

## The shape of the journey

Six phases, ten-ish days each:

1. **Foundations** — the math and the Python toolchain.
2. **Data & Classical ML** — pandas, SQL, scikit-learn, evaluation.
3. **Deep Learning** — PyTorch from first principles to CNNs.
4. **LLMs & GenAI** — transformers in practice: prompting, RAG, fine-tuning.
5. **Agentic AI & MLOps** — tool use, then shipping models properly.
6. **Capstone** — one end-to-end project from framing to deployment.

<Callout kind="tip" title="How to use this site">
  Each day links to the exact math lessons and papers you need *that day*. You
  don't have to finish a math course before Day 1 — you learn the math when a
  model demands it.
</Callout>

## Why math is non-negotiable

Take gradient descent — the engine under nearly everything you'll train. The
update rule is:

$$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \, \nabla_\theta J(\theta_t)$$

That one line uses derivatives (calculus), vectors and matrices (linear
algebra), and a loss function $J(\theta)$ you'll learn to read statistically.
Every phase of the roadmap points back to the [mathematics section](/math) at
the moment you need it.

## The contract

<Callout kind="info" title="The rules">
  1. One roadmap day per day. Don't binge, don't skip.
  2. Every exercise gets typed into a real notebook — no mental execution.
  3. When a day names a paper, skim it before moving on. Summaries help; the
  original teaches pattern recognition for the literature.
</Callout>

Check your streak on the [roadmap page](/roadmap) — progress is stored in your
browser, so it follows you on that machine.
