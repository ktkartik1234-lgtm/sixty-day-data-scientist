Every model this phase learned from labels. Today, structure without any —
clusters and principal components — and three honest ways they earn their
place in a supervised pipeline. The geometry underneath is the
**eigendecomposition of a covariance matrix** — vectors that only stretch,
never rotate.

## k-means in three steps

Initialize k centroids, assign each point to its nearest centroid, move each
centroid to its cluster's mean — repeat until assignments stop changing. The
loop is locally minimizing the **inertia**:

$$\min_{C} \sum_{i} \lVert x_i - \mu_{c(i)} \rVert_2^2$$

```python
from sklearn.cluster import KMeans

km = KMeans(n_clusters=4, n_init=10, random_state=0).fit(Xs)
labels = km.labels_
```

`n_init=10` reruns from ten random starts and keeps the best — cheap
insurance against a bad initialization.

## Choosing k: elbow and silhouette

Inertia always falls as k rises, so its plot only *suggests* an elbow where
returns diminish. The silhouette scores each point against its own cluster
versus the nearest other — roughly a shape quality between minus one and one:

$$s(i) = \frac{b(i) - a(i)}{\max\left( a(i),\, b(i) \right)}$$

with $a(i)$ the mean distance to its own cluster and $b(i)$ to the nearest
other cluster:

```python
from sklearn.metrics import silhouette_score

for k in range(2, 9):
    km = KMeans(n_clusters=k, n_init=10, random_state=0).fit(Xs)
    print(k, round(km.inertia_, 1), round(silhouette_score(Xs, km.labels_), 3))
```

## PCA: rotate, then truncate

On standardized data, PCA finds the orthogonal directions of maximal
variance — the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix, ordered by eigenvalue:

$$\Sigma v_j = \lambda_j v_j$$

Keep the top components and you keep most of the information in far fewer
dimensions; `inverse_transform` reconstructs a denoised approximation:

```python
from sklearn.decomposition import PCA

evr = PCA().fit(Xs).explained_variance_ratio_
cumulative = np.cumsum(evr)
n95 = int(np.argmax(cumulative >= 0.95)) + 1
print(n95, "components explain 95% of variance")
plt.plot(range(1, len(cumulative) + 1), cumulative, marker="o")
```

## When unsupervised earns its keep

Three honest uses: **segments** — cluster labels become a categorical feature
or a business lens; **compression** — a PCA front-end can denoise and speed
up linear models; **anomalies** — points with large reconstruction error or
far from every centroid are worth a human look. None of these replaces the
supervised pipeline; each feeds it.

<Callout kind="deep" title="Scale is destiny in unsupervised learning">
  k-means minimizes Euclidean distances and PCA maximizes Euclidean variance,
  so a feature measured in tens of thousands dominates both regardless of its
  relevance — age in years barely exists. Standardize first, then interpret
  components against the *scaled* units, and remember both are linear
  methods: two Gaussians on a ring will outwit k-means no matter how you
  scale them.
</Callout>
