Yesterday the models learned to see; today they learn to paint. Diffusion
generates by destroying images and learning to undo the destruction — the
same recipe now powers audio, video and molecule design.

## Forward process: noise on a schedule

Corrupt data one small Gaussian step at a time:

$$q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\left(x_t;\ \sqrt{1 - \beta_t}\, x_{t-1},\ \beta_t \mathbf{I}\right)$$

The schedule's Gaussian steps compose into a closed form — you can jump to
any timestep $t$ directly from $x_0$, without walking the chain:

$$x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\, \epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I}), \quad \bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t} (1 - \beta_s)$$

```python
import numpy as np

x0 = np.random.randn(28, 28)                 # pretend this is an image
T = 1000
betas = np.linspace(1e-4, 0.02, T)           # noise schedule
alpha_bar = np.cumprod(1 - betas)            # signal energy remaining

def q_sample(x0, t, eps=None):
    eps = np.random.randn(*x0.shape) if eps is None else eps
    return np.sqrt(alpha_bar[t]) * x0 + np.sqrt(1 - alpha_bar[t]) * eps, eps

for t in (10, 100, 999):
    print(f"t={t}: signal fraction {alpha_bar[t]:.4f}")
```

By $t = T$ the signal fraction is effectively zero: pure noise.

## Reverse process: a network that predicts the noise

Training is plain regression. Draw a sample, corrupt it at a random $t$, and
teach a U-Net to name the exact noise that was added:

$$\mathcal{L}_{simple} = \mathbb{E}_{x_0, t, \epsilon} \left\lVert \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) \right\rVert^2$$

Sampling reverses the corruption: start from pure noise and repeatedly
subtract the predicted noise, step by step, for $T$ steps. Smarter samplers
such as DDIM cut that to 20–50 steps with little quality loss.

## Text conditioning and guidance

Text enters through cross-attention: the denoiser attends to text embeddings
at every layer, so each denoising step is steered by the prompt.
Classifier-free guidance sharpens the steer — train with the caption dropped
10–20 percent of the time, then at sampling time extrapolate *away from* the
unconditional prediction:

$$\tilde{\epsilon} = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \emptyset) + w \left( \epsilon_\theta(x_t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \emptyset) \right)$$

A guidance weight $w$ above 1 trades output diversity for prompt fidelity —
the dial behind "creative" versus "literal" image generation.

## Latent diffusion: the speed trick

Pixels are enormous: a 512 x 512 x 3 image is over 780,000 numbers. The
latent diffusion insight is to run the whole diffusion process in the
compressed latent space of a pretrained VAE — 64 x 64 x 4 — roughly 48x
fewer numbers per step — and decode back to pixels only at the end. That is
the Stable Diffusion paper's trick, and it is why generation moved from
clusters to gaming GPUs.

<Callout kind="deep" title="One idea, every modality">
  The DDPM recipe is modality-agnostic: corrupt with a schedule, train a
  network to predict the corruption, sample by reversing. Waveforms, protein
  structures, video frames — only the denoiser architecture and the schedule
  change. When the next generative modality appears, look for the diffusion
  under it.
</Callout>
